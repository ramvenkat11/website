Review https://search2o.com as a first-time visitor and tell me how well it
persuades someone who tries it. I built the site, so be blunt.

Use a real browser, not WebFetch or curl. The right side of the homepage holds
three live demos that are drawn by script, so a text fetch shows none of them.
Use the connected Chrome browser; if it isn't connected, use Playwright with
Chromium and install it if needed.

Background: Search2o lets a company build hundreds of task-specific AI agents;
a search picks and runs the right agent for each request. The agents live on
the Search2o agent server, not in the assistant's context. That is what the
hero diagram shows and what "Avoid context rot" refers to. The demos run live
against a shared account with 200 agents that return mock output.

What to do:
1. Take a full-page screenshot at a desktop width (about 1440px) and a phone
   width (about 390px), and look at both. Note what a visitor sees first, and
   whether the demos are visible without scrolling at each width.
2. For each of the three demos, in order: screenshot it before touching it,
   and say what a newcomer would think it is and what they would do first.
   Then use it the way a curious developer would, without reading the docs.
   - If it takes requests: try the suggested input if there is one, a request
     of your own that one of the 200 agents should handle, a vague request
     that could fit several agents, a request no agent could handle, and a
     follow-up that depends on the previous answer.
   - If it builds or generates something: give it two descriptions of your
     own, one simple and one awkward.
   - If it is view-only: click everything that looks clickable.
   Screenshot each result. Record how long each response took, what it showed
   about which agent was chosen, and any error, dead end or confusing state.
   Keep it to about ten runs per demo.
3. Follow "Try it free" and read the page it leads to. Don't submit the
   account form.

What to report:
- For each demo: what it shows, what worked, and what confused or failed,
  each tied to a screenshot, a timing or quoted text.
- Whether the demos prove the headline's three claims: many agents, search
  runs the right one, no context rot.
- The moment a visitor is most likely to leave, and the moment most likely to
  convince them.
- The three changes that would do the most, most important first.

Rules:
- Every criticism needs something you observed. If you couldn't see or run
  something, say so instead of guessing.
- Say what works as plainly as what doesn't.
- The audience is technical early adopters: programmers, AI engineers, Hacker
  News readers. They want to know what it does and how it differs, quickly.
  Don't recommend testimonials, customer logos, trust badges or benefit-style
  marketing copy.
- Judge wording by how a reader will interpret it. Ignore typos.
- Don't critique product design choices on taste, for example agents defined
  in JSON. Report only what you saw happen.
